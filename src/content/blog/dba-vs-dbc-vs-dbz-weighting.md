Switch the weighting control on a sound meter from A to C to Z and the number changes — sometimes by a lot. That's not a bug. Each setting measures the same sound through a different filter, designed for a different purpose. Here's how to choose.

## Why weighting exists

Human hearing is not equally sensitive to all frequencies. We hear midrange sounds (roughly 1–5 kHz, the range of speech) far more readily than very low or very high frequencies. A microphone, by contrast, picks up energy across the spectrum more evenly.

Frequency weighting bridges that gap. It applies a standardized curve (defined in IEC 61672) that boosts or cuts each frequency band before the level is calculated, so the final number better reflects a particular goal.

## A-weighting (dBA)

**A-weighting** mimics the sensitivity of human hearing at moderate levels. It rolls off low frequencies steeply and trims the very top end, emphasizing the midrange where our ears — and speech — live.

- **Use it for:** almost everything related to hearing risk, noise nuisance, workplace exposure and environmental noise.
- **Why:** virtually every standard and regulation, including the NIOSH and OSHA exposure limits, is specified in dBA. If you only ever use one weighting, use this one.

## C-weighting (dBC)

**C-weighting** is much flatter than A. It keeps far more low-frequency energy, only gently rolling off the extreme ends of the spectrum.

- **Use it for:** loud, bass-heavy sound (concerts, clubs, cinema), peak measurements, and checking whether low-frequency rumble is significant.
- **A useful trick:** compare dBC and dBA. A large gap between them (C much higher than A) tells you there is a lot of low-frequency energy present — useful when diagnosing rumble or judging whether earplugs will be enough.

## Z-weighting (dBZ)

**Z-weighting** means "zero" weighting — no filter at all. It reports the raw, flat sound level across the meter's full frequency range.

- **Use it for:** technical analysis, acoustic measurement, and situations where you want the unmodified spectrum rather than a hearing-based interpretation.
- **Keep in mind:** Z-weighted numbers are not directly comparable to the dBA limits in health guidance.

## Quick guide

- Worried about hearing or noise complaints? **dBA.**
- Dealing with bass, peaks, or loud venues? **dBC.**
- Doing technical analysis? **dBZ.**
